## Project

Forensic Sky-Robotics aims to predict an impending football foul from player poses and movement, then direct a virtual camera toward the likely interaction. The project folder is:

E:\AIDS\Sem 5\23AID301_Computer_Vision\End Sem Project

Open that folder in Cursor. In its PowerShell terminal, run:

.\.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
python demo.py

The desktop demo lets you choose a clip and view the broadcast with pose overlays alongside a simulated camera view. Outputs are saved under runs\. The main results and reproduction steps are in research_results\REPORT.md and README.md.

## Work completed

- Processed all 726 SportsD FIFA World Cup 2022 clips and all 600 CAS-FD foul/dive clips. Removed duplicate or conflicting clips from model splits after hash and perceptual checks.
- Downloaded and aligned seven FIFA World Cup 2022 second-half broadcasts with StatsBomb event logs. Four matches were used for training, two for validation/model selection, and Argentina–France final for the held-out test.
- Compared seven continuous-match forecasting candidates: logistic regression with three regularization strengths, CatBoost at two depths, a short-clip transfer model, and a causal temporal CNN. Also compared a proximity/closing-speed rule.
- The selected continuous-match model is L2-regularized logistic regression. It did not train for epochs. Its solver limit was 700 iterations. The temporal CNN comparison trained for 220 epochs.
- Built a virtual camera planner with pair targeting, short target retention, collision/occlusion estimates, motion limits, replay video, JSONL telemetry, and incident clip export.
- Evaluated pose estimation on KTH Multiview Football Dataset II, using running and sliding-tackle sequences with three camera views.
- Wrote a research report, figures, prediction files, event-level results, and camera-evaluation files under research_results\.

## Main continuous World Cup final results

The test covered 53.1 minutes and 18 logged fouls. The model raised 15 alerts within the pre-event matching window. One matched a foul, with 14 false alerts and 17 missed fouls.

- Event precision 6.7%
- Event recall 5.6%
- Event F1 0.061
- Matched alert time 1.21 seconds before the event-log timestamp

Visual review of that one matched example showed player contact had already started. So the timing against the event log does not prove the system anticipated first physical contact.

Frame-level results across 6,375 samples, including 47 positive samples:

- Average precision 0.0106
- ROC AUC 0.510
- Accuracy 98.6%, which is misleading because positive frames are rare

The proximity/closing-speed baseline had average precision 0.0071 and matched no events.

## Supplementary foul-versus-dive clip results

On held-out CAS-FD clips (full retained test split, 95 clips), the selected soft-ensemble model at a 1.0 s prefix achieved:

- Accuracy 64.2%
- Balanced accuracy 64.6%
- Precision 60.4%
- Recall 71.1%
- F1 0.653
- Average precision 0.650
- ROC AUC 0.671

This evaluates foul-versus-dive clip classification on the full official split. It does not measure advance prediction on continuous match footage.

## Pose and camera findings

The KTH benchmark showed that the additional close-up pose pass greatly increased pose coverage. On the sliding-tackle sequence it returned a pose on 510 of 690 images, compared with 20 of 690 for the wide-field detector alone. Pose accuracy still degraded during challenging tackle poses.

On 21 reviewed supplementary foul clips, the system produced one alert at least 0.5 seconds before the manually bracketed contact onset. On four reviewed final challenges, there were no such early alerts. The simulated camera showed no consistent visibility improvement over fixed or survey-camera baselines.

Conclusion: The end-to-end prototype, training runs, validation, held-out evaluation, metrics, and demo are complete for the assembled data. The evaluation does not support claiming reliable or high-accuracy pre-contact foul prediction or improved refereeing decisions.
