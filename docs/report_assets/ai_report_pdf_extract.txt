ABSTRACT 
Broadcast football video is single-view and crowded. Referees and operators 
usually see contact after the players have already closed distance. This project builds 
a computer-vision pipeline that reads pose and short-horizon motion, scores foul-like 
challenges against dive-like patterns on short clips, and moves a simulated aerial 
camera toward the threatened pair when an alert fires. The work is for Computer 
Vision (23AID301). 
The primary evaluation uses the CAS-FD foul-versus-dive collection after 
duplicate filtering. The retained split has 351 train, 98 validation, and 95 held-out test 
clips. Features are causal: only past frames enter the 27-dimensional pair vectors and 
the 81-dimensional pooled scene vector. Model and cutoff selection use validation 
only. Two locked clip results are reported. Step 1 is a CatBoost boosted-deep model 
at a 1.0 s prefix. Step 2 is a soft ensemble at the same cutoff. On the 95-clip test set 
the final ensemble reaches 64.2% accuracy, 64.6% balanced accuracy, 60.4% 
precision, 71.1% recall, F1 0.653, average precision 0.650, and ROC AUC 0.671. 
A secondary continuous track trains on World Cup second halves and tests on 
Argentina-France. Sample accuracy looks high because of class imbalance, while 
event F1 stays near 0.061 and ROC AUC near 0.51. We treat that track as an open 
difficulty. The main claim stays on the clip results. Supporting checks cover pose 
coverage on KTH Multiview Football II and planned versus survey camera framing. 
A desktop demo ships five sample videos, writes replay and telemetry, and shows a 
live ops dashboard during analysis and replay. 
Keywords: computer vision; pose estimation; foul anticipation; soft ensemble; virtual 
camera; CAS-FD; sports broadcast.

CHAPTER - 1 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Problem background 
Football broadcasts are edited for storytelling. The camera rides wide shots, then 
cuts to close-ups after a challenge has already started. A system that wants to warn 
before contact has a hard time with that edit path. Players occlude each other. Jersey 
colours collide under stadium lights. The ball and the referee wander through the 
frame. A model that waits for a clear tackle pose arrives too late for a useful camera 
move. 
This project treats the problem as two linked tasks. The first is clip-level 
discrimination of foul-like challenges from dive-like patterns using pose and motion 
prefixes. The second is continuous scoring on full halves, with an optional virtual 
skycam that relocates when an alarm fires. The title names the second idea (aerial 
repositioning driven by precursor prediction). For the course claim, the numbers that 
hold up come mainly from the CAS-FD clip track. 
CAS-FD supplies short labelled clips. After hashing and perceptual duplicate 
grouping, 544 clips remain from 600 processed files. The held-out test set has 95 
clips (45 foul, 50 dive). SportsD quiet World Cup segments add ordinary-play 
negatives during training only. Continuous evaluation uses seven FIFA World Cup 
2022 second halves aligned to StatsBomb Foul Committed times. 
1.2 Motivation 
Course work in Computer Vision often stops at detection accuracy. Sports 
production also asks where the camera should look next. If a model can name a 
threatened pair a second early, a planned viewpoint can reduce body blockage on the

event metrics. Visual review of the single matched continuous alert shows contact 
already underway, so clock lead alone is not treated as proof of physiological 
anticipation.

CHAPTER - 2 
LITERATURE REVIEW 
2.1 Pose estimation in team sports 
Team-sport pose work has moved from pictorial structures and part detectors to 
deep keypoint models that run at interactive speeds. YOLO-family pose heads give a 
practical compromise: one forward pass yields boxes and joints for several players. 
MediaPipe Pose Landmarker supplies denser 3D landmarks once a box is known, 
which helps display and limb-motion estimates even when the detector cadence stays 
at a few hertz. 
Multiview datasets such as KTH Multiview Football II show that a close-up 
stream recovers limbs that a wide broadcast loses. Sliding tackles remain hard 
because limbs blur and bodies overlap. Our pose supporting study repeats that 
pattern: coverage rises when close-up detections merge with wide-field tracks, while 
tackle sequences still lag. 
2.2 Action and foul recognition 
Foul recognition literature often uses trimmed clips with explicit labels. CAS-FD 
frames the problem as foul versus dive, which matches a practical dispute in match 
review. Spatiotemporal CNNs and transformer video models can score whole clips, 
yet they hide which players drove the decision and they need heavy compute for class 
projects. Pose graphs and handcrafted kinematic features stay inspectable: separation, 
closing speed, and limb extension map to language a referee already uses. 
We keep the clip track on tabular learners (boosted trees, histograms, logistic 
members) over cached pose features. That choice makes threshold selection and 
ensemble averaging easy to audit against validation AP.

Anticipation means scoring an event before its annotated time using only past 
evidence. In continuous football halves the positive window is thin. A 0.75-2.00 s 
lead before StatsBomb Foul Committed yields few positives per minute. Methods that 
look strong on balanced clip sets often collapse when every second of ordinary play 
becomes a negative. Alarm logic then matters: refractory intervals and rearms after a 
below-threshold observation stop a model from chattering. 
Our continuous protocol selects by event F1 under a ceiling of one false alert per 
minute on validation halves, then freezes the threshold for ARG-FRA. The resulting 
event F1 of 0.061 and AUC near 0.51 sit with other hard sports anticipation baselines. 
They do not match the clip numbers. 
2.4 Camera planning for sports 
Automated cameramen optimise framing for ball or player clusters. Less work 
ties camera choice to foul precursors. A planned skycam can search viewpoints that 
keep a sightline clear of intervening bodies. Cost terms usually mix blockage, travel 
distance, and pair separation projected into the view. Smooth motion limits stop the 
camera from teleporting between optima each frame. 
In this project the camera module samples candidate positions around the 
predicted pair, ranks them by blockage and separation, and integrates velocity toward 
the chosen destination. On reviewed contact brackets the planned view did not beat 
survey or fixed baselines consistently, largely because early alerts were rare. The 
planner still gives the demo a concrete relocate behaviour when the clip alarm fires. 
2.5 Summary of gaps 
Published foul classifiers rarely ship a causal prefix protocol with frozen cutoffs 
and a full confusion table on a stated test split. Continuous anticipation papers 
sometimes lead with sample accuracy that imbalance inflates. Camera papers seldom 
connect alerts to a relocate path with logged telemetry. This repository puts those

3.3 Perception and features 
Detection uses YOLO11n-pose. A tracker maintains identities across the analysis 
cadence (2 Hz for clip feature caching). Shot-cut resets clear tracks when histogram 
signatures jump. Close-player detections merge with wide tracks for display. For each 
nearby pair (up to eight), the system builds a 27-D vector from separation, approach 
or closing speed, relative motion, limb angles and extension, joint confidence, and 
short-horizon temporal change. An 81-D scene vector pools those pairs. No future 
frames, event labels, filenames, or scoreboard clocks enter as numeric inputs. 
MediaPipe supplies hip-relative 3D landmarks inside player boxes when 
confidence allows. Leg-motion magnitudes feed the camera focus heuristic during 
alerts. Missing 3D pose falls back to a vertical stick figure in the skycam view. 
3.4 Clip classification protocol 
Clips are decoded and pose or pair states are cached at 2 Hz. A prefix sample is 
taken at cutoff times 1.0, 1.5, or 2.0 s. Training uses train-split prefixes with multi-
time augmentation. Validation alone selects the model family, cutoff, and decision 
threshold. The threshold raises the worst of accuracy, balanced accuracy, F1, 
precision, and recall on validation. Final selection among boosted and soft-ensemble 
families stays inside a near-best validation AP band. The 95-clip test set is evaluated 
once. 
Step 1 locks CatBoost boosted-deep at 1.0 s. Step 2 locks soft-ensemble 
(probability average of CatBoost, tree, and logistic members) at 1.0 s with threshold 
approximately 0.328. Integrity rules forbid test-set hyperparameter fitting.

Candidates fit on four training halves. Validation halves choose model and 
threshold by event F1 under at most one false alert per minute, breaking ties by 
average precision. The selected model is L2-regularised logistic regression with C = 
0.0005 and threshold 0.018414. ARG-FRA is tested once. Alarms rearm after a 
below-threshold observation and keep a two-second refractory interval. Time gaps 
and shot changes reset state. 
3.6 Virtual camera control 
When a pair is held under alert, the camera samples destinations on a ring around 
the mean future position of the pair at two heights. Each candidate pays for blockage 
along the sightline, travel distance from the current xyz, and gains credit for 
separating the two targets in the projected view. Velocity integrates toward the 
lowest-cost destination under acceleration limits. Without an alert the camera surveys 
a wide orbit behind the median player cluster. Trails and destinations are logged for 
the dashboard map.

CHAPTER - 5 
CONCLUSION 
5.1 Summary of findings 
On the full held-out CAS-FD test set the locked soft-ensemble classifier reaches 
about 64% accuracy, 65% F1, 71% recall, 60% precision, 65% AP, and 67% ROC 
AUC at a 1.0 s prefix. The Step-1 boosted-deep model already cleared the 60% band; 
Step 2 improves most classification metrics further. Continuous foul anticipation on 
ARG-FRA stays near chance on event metrics and should be presented as an open 
challenge. Pose coverage benefits from close-up fusion. Planned camera gains were 
inconsistent on reviewed events. 
5.2 Contributions 
6. A causal pose-feature pipeline with validation-only model and threshold 
selection for foul-versus-dive clips. 
7. Two locked result sets (boosted-deep and soft-ensemble) with full tables, 
curves, and confusion counts. 
8. An honest continuous World Cup evaluation with sample and event metrics 
side by side. 
9. A virtual skycam planner wired to alerts, plus a desktop demo with live ops 
dashboard and five runnable samples. 
5.3 Future work 
Stronger continuous models will need better positive mining and calibrated 
multi-view input. Learned camera policies with visibility rewards are another option. 
Clip performance may rise with temporal transformers over pose tracks if compute 
allows, provided the same validation-only discipline remains. A calibrated pitch
